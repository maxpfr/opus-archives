Opus Archives acquires licences of use on the internal documentation of digital companies. Once anonymized, this content is used only to train and evaluate artificial intelligence models. We do not buy the software.

Who it is for

A digital company produces rich documentation every day that is only ever used internally. It can be given value at several points in the life of the company.

01

Operating company

Documentation built up over years can generate revenue without giving up anything of your business: the licence is non-exclusive and you keep ownership of your content and your software.

02

Voluntary wind-down

In a dissolution or solvent liquidation, documentation becomes an asset with value, provided it is exported before online accounts are closed.

03

Divestment or product shutdown

When a business line is sold or a product discontinued, its documentation can be valued separately, in line with any commitments made elsewhere.

04

Judicial liquidation

With the liquidator and on authorization of the supervising judge: an asset usually worth nothing in the procedure becomes revenue for creditors.

What we buy, and what we do not

We acquire a non-exclusive licence of use on clearly defined written content. The scope is set out in writing before anything is signed.

What we license

  • Wikis and Notion or Confluence spaces
  • Tickets: Jira, Linear, GitHub Issues
  • Specifications and design documents
  • Runbooks and postmortems
  • Code repository history, treated as data: commits, pull requests, reviews, links to tickets

What we exclude

  • The software itself, the brand and the domain names
  • Messaging: Slack, Teams, email
  • Customer files and production databases
  • HR and legal spaces
  • Personal data of customers and employees
  • Code written for clients or under a third-party licence

Ownership and exploitation of the software, brand and domain names remain entirely with the company, which stays free to sell them. We acquire no right to run, distribute or commercialize the software, nor to build a competing product.

How it works

The steps depend on the company's situation. Choose your case.

Companies

  1. First conversation

    Initial contact and short qualification: tools used, type of content, specific constraints.

  2. Inventory under NDA

    Review of tools, volume and age of the content, under a non-disclosure agreement.

  3. Written proposal

    A proposal setting out the exact scope of the licensed content and the list of exclusions.

  4. Licence agreement

    Signature of a non-exclusive licence of use, limited to training and evaluating AI models.

  5. Export and anonymization

    Export of the content, anonymization, then deletion of the raw exports.

What you get

  • Revenue from an asset that already exists
  • No transfer of ownership: your content and your software remain yours
  • Scope and exclusions agreed in writing
  • Export and anonymization carried out by us

Insolvency practitioners

  1. Case identification

    Identification of the company concerned and short qualification with the practitioner's office.

  2. Inventory under NDA

    Review of tools, volume and age of the content, under a non-disclosure agreement.

  3. Firm offer

    Offer in the usual French format: 10% deposit by bank cheque, bank certificate of funds, declaration of independence (art. L642-3 of the French Commercial Code), no conditions precedent.

  4. Authorization and deed

    Authorization by the supervising judge (juge-commissaire), then signature of the deed.

  5. Export and anonymization

    Export of the content, anonymization, then deletion of the raw exports.

For the procedure

  • An asset usually worth nothing in the procedure becomes revenue
  • Export, anonymization and deed costs are borne by us
  • A protective export can be run before online accounts are closed for unpaid subscriptions
  • The software, brand and domain names stay in the estate and remain free to be sold

Data protection

Confidentiality is a precondition of the business. Every export follows the same protocol.

Pseudonymized authors

Authors' names, usernames and addresses are replaced with pseudonyms.

Secrets removed

Keys, tokens and technical credentials are detected and removed.

Personal data removed

Customer names and personal data found in the text are removed.

Raw exports deleted

Original exports are deleted once the anonymized data has been delivered.

Limited, documented access

Only the people who need it can access exports, and every access is logged.

For AI developers

We build datasets drawn from real work: documentation, tickets, code reviews and the links between them. They are designed for training, evaluation and building reinforcement-learning environments.

  • Rights-cleared: every dataset rests on a written licence of use
  • Anonymized under a documented protocol
  • Traceable: the provenance of each dataset is tied to its licence

Frequently asked questions

Do you buy the software?

No. We only acquire a non-exclusive licence of use on the documentation. The software, brand and domain names remain the company's property, and it stays free to sell them. We acquire no right to run, distribute or commercialize the software.

Do you collect employees' messaging or emails?

No. Slack, Teams and email are excluded from the scope, as are HR and legal spaces, customer files and production databases.

What happens to personal data?

Authors are pseudonymized, customer names and personal data found in the text are removed, and raw exports are deleted after delivery.

What is the content used for?

Only to train and evaluate artificial intelligence models. It is not used to build a competing product.

Is the licence exclusive?

No. It is non-exclusive: the company keeps its content and can continue to use it as it wishes.

The online accounts are about to be closed. Is it too late?

Not necessarily. In a liquidation, we can run a protective export before accounts are closed for unpaid subscriptions. Contact us as early as possible.
